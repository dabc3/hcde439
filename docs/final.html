Final Project


Starry light is an visual sensory tool that helps children fall asleep easier. It activates when a light sensor detects when the room is dark, and spins a lampshade with hole cut outs to mimic stars! To the right you will see a gif of my operating prototype. My circuit contains a photoresistor that senses the brightness of the room. When the lights turn off, the multicolor LED begins fading in between colors and the stepper motor begins spinning. I 3D printed a lampshade to go on top of the stepper motor, so when the motor spins the lamspade spins, projecting moving stars across the walls of the room. When the photoresistor senses that the lights turn back on, the LED turns off and the stepper motor stops moving, making the lamp appear as if it turned off.

Did you use AI tools in completing this assignment?
I used AI to explain the timing issue that I was having between the LED fading and the photoresistor sensing the brightness in the room. Other than that all work was my own.

Calculations:

I used the equation V=IR rearranged to R=V/I to find the resistor needed for the red LED. Voltage input is 5V from the source, and the voltage drop is 2V for the red led, leaving us with 3V over 20mA. I got 150 ohms from my calculations, but was told to round to 220 ohms in class for safety. The joystick does not need a resistor.

Schematic:

I created these schematics using icons learned in class. The power source is 5V for the multicolored led, the photoresistor, and the stepper motor. The resistor for the LED should be 220 ohms for the red and green, and 100 ohms for the blue based on my calculations and rounding. The photoresistor uses a 10k ohm resistor.
